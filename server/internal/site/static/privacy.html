Wird

Privacy

Last updated 3 October 2026

Wird works without an account. Signed out, everything you read, mark and keep stays on your phone, and Wird sends nothing about you anywhere. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.

The microphone

When you recite, Wird listens through the microphone to follow along. The sound is processed on your phone by a model downloaded once from wird.bnei.dev. It is never recorded, stored or sent anywhere.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional and exists to carry your reading between your devices. You sign in with a bnei.dev account (Authentik), which holds your email address and password. The app shows your email address or username in Settings so you know which account you are on; Wird's server does not store it. Wird then keeps, on its server:

  • an identifier for your account;
  • which ayas you understood and which roots you learned;
  • what you kept: ayas, roots and notes, including the text of your notes;
  • your sets, the prayers you prayed them in and when, and where you stopped reading;
  • your reading order preference.

Some of this, such as the prayers you recorded, says something about your religious practice. It is stored only so it can reach your other devices. It is never shared, sold or used for anything else.

Reports and verdicts

If you send a report from the app, it carries what you wrote, the screen you were on, the app, corpus and senses versions, your platform (Android or iOS) and the app's language. If you say whether a root's meaning reads right, that verdict also carries a short fingerprint of the sentence you judged. Reports and verdicts are not linked to your account. Wird's maintainers may summarise a report in the project's public issue tracker, but never quote it.

Other services the app reaches

  • Recitation audio is played from everyayah.com and, if you turn on word-by-word audio, from audio.qurancdn.com (Quran Foundation). These hosts see your IP address when the audio is fetched, as any web server does.
  • Sign-in goes through authentik.bnei.dev, only if you choose to sign in.
  • Dictionaries. Under a root, “Look this root up in the dictionaries” opens the Arabic Almanac (ejtaal.net) in your browser. Wird sends nothing itself; the site sees your IP address and which root you looked up, and runs its own visitor statistics.

Deleting your account

In the app, open Settings, then Account, then Delete account. This deletes your Wird account and everything synced to it from the server, and empties your phone. It cannot be undone.

You can also ask by email at wird@bnei.dev, from the address on your account, and it will be done within 30 days.

After a deletion the server keeps only a one-way hash of your account identifier and the time of deletion, so a phone still holding your old sign-in cannot recreate the account. Your bnei.dev sign-in itself is shared with other bnei.dev services and is not deleted with Wird; ask by email if you want it gone too.
